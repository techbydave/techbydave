The [Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen)](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Bose+QuietComfort+Headphones+%282nd+Gen%29&tag=techtreck02-20) launched in August 2026 at $359, and that was a hard sell. At $199 on Amazon, they're a good buy. Three colors hit that price. The rest are $219.

## Quick verdict: buy at $199 to $219, skip at $359

At $199 to $219, these are the easy recommendation for anyone who wants comfortable noise-cancelling headphones without paying flagship money. At the $359 list price, skip them. Reviewers were lukewarm at that number, and the discount is what fixes it. One catch: if you only care about noise cancelling and want to save more, the original model is cheaper still (more on that below).

## What changed from the first-gen QuietComfort

Bose's own comparison says the 2nd Gen keeps the original's noise cancelling, comfort and Quiet and Aware modes. It adds Bose TrueSpatial spatial audio, Cinema and Immersion modes, and lossless audio over USB. Plugged into a compatible device, the headphones skip Bluetooth compression. The ecoustics spec list puts USB audio at up to 24-bit/48kHz.

The rest of the changes:

- **Noise cancelling:** six microphones and updated adaptive feedback control. ecoustics says that helps most with steady low-frequency noise like dishwashers and aircraft engines.
- **Bluetooth:** version 5.4, up from 5.1 on the old model, per Tom's Guide. Codecs are still just AAC and SBC.
- **Comfort:** Tom's Guide says the padding now runs almost fully around the headband. The previous generation only padded the top quarter.
- **Design:** pill-shaped earcups and a sliding headband, plus two-tone colors.
- **Battery:** 24 hours with ANC, and 18 hours with the immersive modes on, according to What Hi-Fi. Fifteen minutes of charging gives 2.5 hours, per Bose Canada.

Tom's Guide gave them 3.5 stars and dinged them for no wear detection and mediocre call quality. What Hi-Fi's headline was that the noise cancelling is strong but rivals sound better. It points to the [Sennheiser Momentum 5 Wireless](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Sennheiser+Momentum+5+Wireless&tag=techtreck02-20), which runs 57 hours on a charge.

## Who should buy these, and who should skip

Buy them if comfort is your priority. Reviewers keep coming back to the plush earcups and fit, and the 2nd Gen is described as the most wearable Bose over-ear yet. They suit commuters, desk workers and anyone upgrading from a pair of QuietComfort 35s or older. The spatial audio modes are a bonus for movies and music on a phone or laptop.

Skip them if you:

- Want the best sound. What Hi-Fi says rivals do it better.
- Travel a lot and want battery life. 24 hours is average, and the Momentum 5 Wireless nearly triples it.
- Take lots of calls. Tom's Guide marked call quality down.
- Rely on aptX. Neither this model nor Trusted Reviews' complaint-free wish list gets it here. Only the Ultra line adds aptX Adaptive.

## Quick answers

### Are the Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen) better than the first generation?

Yes. Trusted Reviews says there's no doubt they're better, with tighter bass and more consistent noise blocking. You get spatial audio, USB lossless and a newer Bluetooth chip, though the drivers aren't upgraded.

### Do the Bose QuietComfort Headphones (2nd Gen) support aptX?

No. They use AAC and SBC only. The QuietComfort Ultra Headphones (2nd Gen) add aptX Adaptive.
