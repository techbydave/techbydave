The Google [Pixel Tag](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pixel+Tag&tag=techtreck02-20) costs $29.99 for one or $99.99 for a four-pack, and it's a good tracker with one awkward flaw: it has no way to attach to your keys. If you carry an Android phone and want something that slips into a wallet or luggage pocket, buy it. If you mostly want a tag for a key ring, a cheaper round tracker does that job better.

## Quick verdict: buy for wallets and bags, skip for keys

The Pixel Tag is the first tracker Google has built for its own Find Hub network. It's a thin stainless steel tag with UWB for arrow-and-distance finding, a loud speaker, and a replaceable battery. How-To Geek's review calls it the best Android tracker you can buy, then dings it for having no mounting point. That means a keychain case is an extra purchase. The [Moto Tag 2](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Moto+Tag+2&tag=techtreck02-20) matches it on features for less money right now, so the Pixel Tag wins only if its shape suits where you'll carry it.

## What the Pixel Tag actually brings

The tag measures 1.1 by 1.8 by 0.2 inches and weighs 0.4 oz with its battery. It's rated IP67, which covers 1 meter of water for up to 30 minutes. The battery is a user-replaceable CR2032 that Google says lasts over a year.

For finding things close by, it uses Bluetooth 6.0 with Channel Sounding plus ultra-wideband. Find Hub shows visual distance and directional arrows. There's a physical button too. Press it twice and it rings your phone, which is handy if the phone is what's lost. The speaker has three volume settings and tops out around 80 decibels.

Compatibility is wide: Android 9 or newer via Fast Pair. But the precise-finding tricks need a phone with a UWB chip or Bluetooth 6.0 support, according to The Verge. Location can be shared with up to 10 people in Find Hub. Google's store page also advertises "left behind" alerts, which 9to5Google said other trackers don't support yet. TechCrunch notes Google didn't mention NFC at announcement, but the tag has it, so a stranger can tap it to see your contact info.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

**Buy it if:**
- You want a wallet, passport or luggage-pocket tracker. The 0.2-inch body is the point.
- You use a Pixel, or any Android phone with UWB, and want precision finding.
- You like the idea of the double-press button to ring your phone.

**Skip it if:**
- You want a tag for keys and bags with loops. Without a built-in mount, you're adding a case, which pushes the real cost up.
- You're on an iPhone. This only works with Android's Find Hub. Apple's [AirTag 2](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=AirTag+2&tag=techtreck02-20) is the iPhone pick, and the two list at the same $29.99 in the US.
- You'd rather split one tracker between Apple and Google networks. See Pebblebee below.

## Cheaper or better alternatives

**Moto Tag 2, $19.99 on Amazon right now.** Android Authority says it's a circular tag with UWB, a speaker and a CR2032 battery. It's rated IP68 against the Pixel Tag's IP67, and its battery is rated over 600 days. It's also smaller and lighter, and round tags fit existing key-ring accessories. Usually it runs $20 for one, though some listings put it at $29.99, so check the price before you pay. The four-pack has been listed at $69.99 versus the Pixel Tag's $99.99. **[Pebblebee Clip 5](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pebblebee+Clip+5&tag=techtreck02-20), $34.99.** It's rechargeable over USB-C and can be set up on either Find Hub or Apple's Find My, one at a time. The catch: no UWB, so no arrow guidance up close. Pick it for a mixed iPhone and Android household.

**Samsung's [Galaxy SmartTag3](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Galaxy+SmartTag3&tag=techtreck02-20).** 9to5Google says it also launched at $30 each or $25 apiece in a $100 four-pack. Android Authority notes Samsung's tracker is more restrictive about which phones it works with, while the Pixel Tag works across Android.

**Apple AirTag 2.** iPhone only, and the answer if you're on iOS.

My pick for most Android owners: Moto Tag 2 for keys, and one Pixel Tag for the wallet.

## Questions people are asking

### Does the Pixel Tag work with a Samsung Galaxy phone?

Yes. It pairs through Fast Pair on Android 9 or newer. For UWB precision finding, the phone needs a UWB chip or Bluetooth 6.0.

### Is the Pixel Tag better than the Moto Tag 2?

On features they're close, per How-To Geek. The Moto Tag 2 has the edge on water resistance (IP68), battery rating and shape, and it's cheaper at the moment. The Pixel Tag is thinner and has a physical button.

### Does the Pixel Tag need a subscription?

I found no subscription fee listed in any of the sources I checked. It uses the Find Hub network and Android devices nearby to locate items.

If you're buying this week, check the Moto Tag 2's $19 price before you add the Pixel Tag to your cart. And if you do buy the Pixel Tag, grab the $5-off keychain case with it at checkout.
