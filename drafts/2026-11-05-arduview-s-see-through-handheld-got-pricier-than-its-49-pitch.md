The [Arduview](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Arduview&tag=techtreck02-20)'s headline price has drifted. When the handheld was first announced in early September, early backers were offered a $49 founder price for a refundable $5 deposit, with a $100 retail price promised later. By the time the Kickstarter went live, the numbers looked different. A hands-on review at Retro Dodo lists a launch price of HK$573, about $74, and a retail price of HK$1,159, about $148. The Awesomer's pre-order listing starts at $73. That's a lot of creep for a gadget whose main trick is being clear.

**What you actually get**

The trick is a real one, though. Plenty of devices have had clear plastic shells. The Arduview goes further, with a transparent circuit board and a transparent OLED screen as well. It weighs 50 grams, charges over USB-C, and ships with 400 preloaded 8-bit games. Notebookcheck points out that the "world's first" label is the maker's marketing claim and hasn't been independently verified.

The guts are modest on purpose. It runs on the open-source Arduboy platform and an ATmega32u4 microcontroller with 2.5KB of RAM and 32KB of storage. There's no Wi-Fi and no app store. You turn it on and play tiny games.

**The review caveats**

Retro Dodo called it impressive but unfinished. The screen is small, and you mostly see through it by holding it up toward your eye. The reviewer's unit had no working sound, and wanted better buttons. The reviewer said they'd pay the Kickstarter price but not the retail one. For context, the review notes you can already get handhelds that play Game Boy games for about $149.

**Who should care**

If you like transparent electronics as objects, the Kickstarter price is the only version that makes sense, and only if you can live with a long wait. Backer hardware is expected in 2027. Kickstarter pricing and timelines can shift, so don't treat any of this as final.

If you want to actually play things, skip it. The [Arduboy FX-C](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Arduboy+FX-C&tag=techtreck02-20) is the same platform in an opaque case. Tom's Hardware listed it on Amazon at $79.99, and it's available now. Arduboy is open source, so homemade games are the whole point of the ecosystem, and you don't need a see-through case for those.

**What to watch**

Check the Kickstarter page for the final pledge tiers and shipping estimates before you back anything. Look for updates on sound and button quality from people who've received units. If those get fixed and the price holds near $73, it's a fair novelty. If not, wait for reviews of the retail version.
