## What the Micro is, and what changed from the first one

The 2nd Gen is a palm-sized Bluetooth speaker. It's rated for up to 12 hours of battery, charges over USB-C, and uses Bluetooth 5.4 with a range of up to 30 feet. Bose's app gives you an EQ, and Android users get Google Fast Pair. Best Buy Canada's listing also lists multipoint, so it can connect to two devices at once.

Bose rates it IP67 for dust and water resistance. A few deal posts say IP68 or 24 hours of battery. Bose's own pages say IP67 and 12 hours, so go with those.

Compared with the original, the second generation fixes its main weakness, battery life, and adds the Bose app and better Bluetooth. It also swaps the old hook clip for a fabric strap. A review comparison notes the fabric strap replaces the 1st-gen's hook clip, which was prone to breaking. It supports AAC and aptX as well as SBC, plus true wireless stereo pairing. You can link two Micros for stereo, or use Party Mode with other compatible Bose speakers.

One catch is the microphone. Bose's Canadian spec table lists no microphone for the 2nd Gen, and one review says no phone calls and no voice assistant. A couple of Black Friday deal posts mention a built-in mic for calls. I'd trust Bose's spec sheet over those. If speakerphone use matters to you, check before you buy.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

**Buy it if:**
- You want a speaker that fits in a jacket pocket or clips to a bike handlebar or pack.
- You like Bose's smoother, balanced sound. One RTINGS comparison says the Bose and JBL sound fairly similar, with little bass and a balanced mid-range, and that the Bose lasts a little longer off a single charge.
- You own other Bose speakers and want Party Mode.
- You're buying a small gift. At this price, two for stereo is also an option.

**Skip it if:**
- You want the loudest speaker for the money. GymCaddy's hands-on comparison says the [JBL Clip 5](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=JBL+Clip+5&tag=techtreck02-20) is simply going to get louder than the Bose.
- You want calls or a speaker that floats.
- You'd be paying the $129 list price. TechRadar's review scored value at 2.5/5 and said rivals offer far better bang for your buck. That score was for the $129 price, not for $79.
- Bass distortion bothers you. One ranking site says the Bose's bass can overdrive and distort at higher volumes. That's another reason to use the EQ.

Bose rates it IP67, so it's dust-tight and survives immersion in water. It doesn't float. The USB-C port is exposed, so dry it before charging.
