The [ASUS ProArt GR1X](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=ASUS+ProArt+GR1X&tag=techtreck02-20) is a 1.1-liter Windows mini PC that costs $4,499 to start, and for most people it's not worth it yet. If you genuinely need to run large AI models on your own desk, it's a real option. If you're curious about "AI PCs," keep your wallet shut.

Also worth knowing before you click anything: the [MSI EdgeMesa N AI+](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=MSI+EdgeMesa+N+AI%2B&tag=techtreck02-20) now costs less for the same base spec.

## The quick verdict: wait, unless local AI is your job

The ProArt GR1X launched November 18, 2026 at $4,499. That's the 64GB memory, 1TB storage model. Windows Central's review headline says the extra performance is offset by a significantly higher price, and I agree with the framing. Buy it only if local AI or heavy creative work pays for it. Everyone else should wait for price cuts, more models from Acer, Gigabyte and HP, or just buy an RTX Spark laptop.

## What's new: a laptop chip with a desktop power budget

The GR1X uses the full RTX Spark N1X chip with 20 CPU cores, 6,144 GPU cores, and a TDP up to 140W. The laptop version tops out at 80W. Nvidia says these desktops deliver up to one petaflop of AI performance and can run language models with 120 billion parameters locally. Memory goes up to 128GB, shared between CPU and GPU.

Other basics: dual M.2 SSD slots, 10GbE networking, WiFi 7, Bluetooth 5.4, and support for up to four 4K displays. It runs Windows 11 on Arm, so x86 app compatibility is something to check before you pay.

How much faster is it than the laptops? Sources disagree. One report found it up to 21.9% faster than an RTX Spark laptop in AI tests. Another outlet says real-world performance gaps come in at roughly 10% or less. Either way, you're paying a lot for a modest gain over the laptop.

ASUS's US site lists channels including the ASUS eShop, Best Buy, Amazon, Micro Center and Central Computers. Best Buy is running Black Friday deals through Nov. 28, but the GR1X listings I found show full price, not a discount. The only real saving so far is MSI's price cut.

**Canada:** I couldn't confirm a Canadian GR1X price. ASUS Canada's pre-order page prices its RTX Spark laptops at C$3,499 for the ProArt P14 and C$4,999 for the P16, and its table lists the GR1X as arriving later, but the price column is unclear. Check ASUS Canada or Best Buy Canada.

**Europe:** €4,699 to start, up to €9,299.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

**Buy it if:**
- You run local language models or AI agents all day and want to skip per-token cloud costs.
- You want that capability in a Windows box small enough to sit next to a monitor.
- You can use 10GbE networking and multiple 4K displays.

**Skip it if:**
- You mainly want a fast desktop for office work, gaming or general use. Windows Central notes the desktops cost a full $700 more than RTX Spark laptops.
- You rely on x86-only software and haven't tested it on Windows on Arm.
- You're just curious about AI. One review calls the price a drawback alongside the limited ports.

## Cheaper alternatives, and the smarter way to buy

**MSI EdgeMesa N AI+ at $4,399.** Same N1X chip, same 64GB, same 1TB, $100 less than the ASUS. At the top end the MSI offers 4TB of storage, while the ASUS tops out at 2TB.

**An RTX Spark laptop.** These started at $2,599 on October 16, 2026. The catch is that the cheap one has less memory. One comparison puts the base laptop at $2,599.99 with 24GB, with 128GB configurations at $5,899.99 and up. If you don't need 64GB or more, the laptop is the sensible buy.

**[Mac Studio M5 Max](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Mac+Studio+M5+Max&tag=techtreck02-20).** One buying guide lists it from $2,499, with up to 128GB. Another puts the 128GB build at $5,399. You lose CUDA, and one guide says CUDA-first tools lag on macOS. If your tools run on macOS, it's much cheaper per GB of memory.

**[DGX Spark](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=DGX+Spark&tag=techtreck02-20).** Nvidia's Linux developer box has had messy pricing. Some pages say $4,699, others report a jump to $6,950 on October 2. Verify the current price before comparing. It's the pick if you want datacenter-style CUDA on Linux.

## Questions people are asking

### Is the ASUS ProArt GR1X better than the MSI EdgeMesa N AI+?
They share the same chip. On the base 64GB model the MSI is $100 cheaper ($4,399 versus $4,499), so the ASUS needs to win on something you care about, like design or retail availability.

### Is an RTX Spark mini PC faster than an RTX Spark laptop?
Somewhat. The desktop runs the chip at up to 140W, but reported gains range from about 10% to nearly 22%, depending on the source.

### Will the price drop?
Not confirmed, but MSI already cut its price by $300 within a week of the ASUS launch. With Acer, Gigabyte and HP models still to come, more competition is likely. If you can wait, watch MSI's and Best Buy's listings in the weeks after Black Friday.
