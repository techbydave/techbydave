Lomography is celebrating one year of the Lomo MC-A by plating one in real gold. The company has announced two limited-edition versions of the camera for the anniversary, and the gold one comes with brown leather. Both are up for preorder in Lomography's online shop, with shipping estimated for December 2026.

I couldn't confirm a price for either limited edition, so I won't guess. What I can tell you is the standard camera's price: $549.

**What you're actually buying**

The MC-A first arrived in October 2025 as a compact 35mm film camera that mixes point-and-shoot ease with manual control. The gold plating is cosmetic, so the hardware should match the regular model. Here's what that hardware is:

- A fixed 32mm f/2.8 lens with autofocus, plus zone focusing from 0.4m
- Automatic, aperture-priority and fully manual exposure modes
- Shutter speeds from bulb to 1/500s, and manual ISO from 12 to 3200
- A built-in flash, but no accessory shoe
- A rechargeable CR2 battery over USB-C, included in the box

The metal body is a big part of the pitch. Amateur Photographer's review lists it at 332g, and calls it a solid chassis with a reassuring heft. That's a long way from the all-plastic cameras that built Lomography's name.

**Who should care**

The standard MC-A is already one of the pricier modern 35mm cameras. Amateur Photographer notes it undercuts the [Rollei 35AF](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Rollei+35AF&tag=techtreck02-20) and sits at about the same level as the half-frame [Pentax 17](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Pentax+17&tag=techtreck02-20). So $549 is mid-pack for a new film camera, not a bargain.

The gold version is for collectors and people who want a gift that looks the part. If you just want to shoot film, a gold finish buys you nothing the regular camera doesn't already do. It might also cost more, though that isn't confirmed. Since one of the two limited editions came with a free glass filter set, the bundle is worth checking against the standard camera before you pay a premium. Also bear in mind that the standard version has drawn good reviews. TechRadar's reviewer called it the best new film camera you can buy after using it for a few months. That's one opinion, but it's a useful one.

Film cameras aren't a one-time purchase, either. Every roll costs you film and development, so budget for that on top of the body.

**What to do**

If you want the plain MC-A, you don't need to wait for anything. It's already on sale in black and silver. If the gold one tempts you, wait for the actual price on Lomography's shop, and check whether the free accessories are included. Then compare it to the standard model, because a December shipping estimate on a limited run is a reason to decide soon rather than eventually.
