A 200MP periscope telephoto is the headline on the Motorola Signature 27, but the number to watch arrives on Thursday: the price. Motorola has confirmed the phone launches November 5, and it has not said what it costs.

**What's confirmed**

Motorola's teaser says the phone is coming to the US, and the company has put up a US landing page. Android Authority reports the chip is Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6, paired with an in-house cooling system called ArcticMesh. The camera setup includes a 50MP Sony LYTIA 910 main sensor and the 200MP periscope. Audio is tuned by Bang & Olufsen. Nokiamob also lists seven years of OS updates as confirmed.

**What's probably true, but not official**

Spec databases fill in the rest. GSMArena and PhoneArena list a 6.8-inch LTPO AMOLED display at 1264 x 2780 with a 165Hz refresh rate. They also list 12GB of RAM, 256GB of UFS 4.1 storage, Android 17, IP68/IP69 ratings and a 5x optical zoom on the telephoto. Treat all of that as likely rather than settled. Gadget Review points out that battery, storage, price and display details come only from third-party databases so far. Even the battery size disagrees between listings: some say 6,000mAh with 90W charging, GSMArena says 7,000mAh.

A Geekbench listing shows 3,313 single-core and 11,088 multi-core points. It also shows 12GB of RAM and Android 17. That's a pre-launch unit, so it's a hint about performance, not a verdict.

**The price problem**

A European retailer briefly listed the 12GB/512GB model at €1,594.90, which Android Authority converts to roughly $1,773. Motorola hasn't confirmed that, and a retailer listing isn't a price list. One spec site guesses $899 for the US, but that's an estimate on a page marked "coming soon," not a source I'd lean on. The gap between those two numbers is about $900. Nobody should plan around either one yet.

Android Authority also notes that November 5 may not settle whether Americans can buy the phone right away. Motorola's own page just says "coming this November."

**My take**

Motorola is pitching this at Samsung's Ultra phones, and the hardware list backs that up. A top-tier chip, a long update promise and a 200MP zoom lens are what you'd want from a flagship. The 2nm-class silicon and the camera hardware are the interesting part, because Motorola hasn't had a phone like this in the US in a while.

But a flagship is only as good as its price and its availability. If the euro figure carries over to the US, this competes with the best phones from Samsung and Apple on their home turf, and Motorola will have to prove the camera and software hold up. If it lands closer to $900, it gets much more interesting.

**What to do**

If you're shopping for a high-end Android phone this season, don't buy anything before Thursday. Check three things when Motorola announces it: the US price for the 12GB/256GB model, the actual on-sale date, and the real battery capacity. Then wait for reviews, especially camera comparisons, before preordering.
