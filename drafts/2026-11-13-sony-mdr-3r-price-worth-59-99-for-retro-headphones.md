Sony's new MDR-3R costs $59.99 in the US, and it's a good-looking way to overpay for wired headphones. Unless the 1979 Walkman styling is the whole point for you, skip it.

The MDR-3R is a modern rebuild of the MDR-3 on-ears that shipped with the original TPS-L2 cassette Walkman. Sony announced it on November 5, 2026. It keeps the slim adjustable metal headband and the open design. It also keeps the 23mm driver size, though Sony says the driver was re-engineered. The foam earpads come in blue and orange, and both pairs are in the box. They weigh about 38g without the cable.

**What changed**

The old 3.5mm plug is gone. In its place is a fixed USB-C cable with an in-line remote and a microphone. There's no battery and no Bluetooth pairing. It works with phones, tablets and computers that support USB Audio Class 1.0. If your phone still has a headphone jack, this won't plug into it.

Then there's the odd detail. Sony says the MDR-3R isn't recommended for use with Walkman players. A retro headphone you shouldn't pair with the retro player is a funny bit of marketing.

**The catch**

The open design leaks sound, and Sony admits it. Coverage also points out that the $59.99 price is high for wired on-ears with limited isolation. Nobody has published measured audio specs yet, so how it sounds is an open question.

The Stranger Things Edition costs $79.99. Sony doesn't identify any hardware difference, so the extra $20 buys branding and packaging. One report says Sony's US page showed the standard model out of stock, so check before you count on getting one. Sony says it's sold through its own store, Amazon and Best Buy.

For Canada, one report citing Sony's release lists C$99.99. I couldn't confirm that figure in a second source, so check Sony Canada before you trust it.

**The smarter buy**

[Apple EarPods](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Apple+EarPods&tag=techtreck02-20) with USB-C list at $19, and Walmart has shown them at $16.99. They have a microphone and remote, and CNN Underscored picked them as its best budget wired earbuds under $20. If you want something more tuned, TechRadar has recommended the [JBL Tune 310C](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=JBL+Tune+310C&tag=techtreck02-20) at around $25, though that price comes from an older piece. In-ear buds will also isolate better than an open on-ear that leaks.

Buy the MDR-3R if you're a Walkman nostalgist who wants a desk or commute novelty and won't be bothered by the leakage. Everyone else should save about $40.

Before you buy, wait for the first independent measurements and reviews. Also check that your phone or laptop supports USB audio over USB-C.
